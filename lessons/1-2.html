Storage vs compute

Snowflake keeps the data in one place and does the thinking somewhere else. Learn why that split is the single most important idea on your project.

12 min Beginner Practice worksheet + 3 challenges 1 lab

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Explain the difference between storage and compute in one sentence
  • Name the three layers of Snowflake and what each one does
  • Say why a bank pays two separate bills and how each one grows
  • Prove in a real account that data stays put while compute is switched off

Definition

Key definition

Storage is where the data sits. Compute is the power that reads it and works out answers. In Snowflake the two are separate, so each one can grow, shrink and be paid for on its own.

In older databases the two were glued together. One machine held the disks and did the thinking. If you needed more disk space you bought a bigger machine, even when the thinking part was idle. Snowflake cuts that link.

  • Storage: your tables, kept in cheap cloud storage. It is always there, even when nobody is working.
  • Compute: the virtual warehouses that run your SQL. They switch on when needed and off when not.
  • Cloud services: the brain that connects them. It checks who you are, plans each query and remembers where every piece of data lives.

Think of it like shelves and librarians

Back to the library in the sky. The books sit on shelves. The shelves do not answer questions; they just hold books, and you pay a small rent for every metre of shelf you fill. The librarians are the ones who run around finding answers. You pay them by the minute, and when nobody is asking anything they go home. Hiring more librarians never means building more shelves, and buying more shelves never means hiring more librarians. At the front desk a manager keeps the master catalogue: they know which shelf holds which book, decide which librarians take which question and check your library card at the door.

In the libraryShelvesIn Snowflake: storage, the cloud disks holding your tables
In the libraryLibrariansIn Snowflake: compute, the virtual warehouses that run queries
In the libraryFront-desk managerIn Snowflake: the cloud services layer that plans queries and keeps the catalogue

Every team can send its own librarians to the same shelves at the same time. Nobody has to copy the books first. That is the whole trick.

The three layers

Snowflake calls this its three-layer architecture. You will see this picture in every Snowflake deck on your project, so learn the names now.

LayerWhat it doesLibraryHow you pay
Database storageHolds every table, compressed and split into small pieces called micro-partitionsShelves with books packed in labelled boxesPer terabyte per month
Query processingVirtual warehouses run your SQL. Each warehouse is its own team and never shares power with anotherTeams of librariansCredits per second while running
Cloud servicesLogs you in, checks permissions, plans and optimises queries, keeps metadata and the result cacheFront-desk managerFree unless it goes above 10% of your daily compute
Good to know

The front-desk manager remembers recent answers. If you run exactly the same query again and the data has not changed, Snowflake hands back the saved result from the cloud services layer instead of sending the librarians out again. That saved answer is kept for 24 hours and stretched to at most 31 days if people keep reusing it.

In practice: a bank

A global bank keeps its card payments in one large database. Four teams read it, and each has its own warehouse:

TeamTheir warehouseWhat they do with the payments dataWhen
FinanceFINANCE_WHMonthly totals per regionOffice hours
FraudFRAUD_WHThousands of tiny checks per hourAll day and night
RiskRISK_WHOne giant month-end modelLast 3 days of the month
MarketingMARKETING_WHOccasional campaign listsRarely

The storage bill is the same whichever team reads the data, because the shelves are shared. The compute bill is different for each team: Fraud’s warehouse runs almost all day, Marketing’s barely runs at all. On the 28th of the month Risk switches on a very large warehouse for a few hours and switches it off again. Nobody else notices.

The SQL you will see

Creating storage and creating compute are two different commands. Notice that neither one mentions the other.

-- storage: a database is a building, the table is a shelf
CREATE DATABASE payments_db;
CREATE TABLE payments_db.public.card_payments (
  payment_id NUMBER, card_no VARCHAR, amount NUMBER, paid_on DATE);

-- compute: a warehouse is a team of librarians
CREATE WAREHOUSE finance_wh WITH WAREHOUSE_SIZE = XSMALL
  AUTO_SUSPEND = 60 AUTO_RESUME = TRUE;

-- point your session at a team, then ask the question
USE WAREHOUSE finance_wh;
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM payments_db.public.card_payments;

Two commands report on the two layers. SHOW WAREHOUSES lists your teams and whether each is started or suspended. SHOW TABLES lists your shelves with their row counts and size in bytes, and it works even when every warehouse is switched off, because the front-desk manager keeps that catalogue.

Lab: switch compute off and watch the data stay

Create a warehouse and a database, then separate them

About 15 minUses your free trial

  1. Open a new SQL worksheet in Snowsight and create a small team of librarians. The settings send them on break after 60 seconds of quiet and bring them back on the next query.

    CREATE WAREHOUSE learn_wh WITH WAREHOUSE_SIZE = XSMALL
  AUTO_SUSPEND = 60 AUTO_RESUME = TRUE;

    Expected: Warehouse LEARN_WH successfully created. If you already made it in an earlier session, use USE WAREHOUSE learn_wh; instead.

  2. Now build some shelves. This creates a database and copies the 150,000 sample customers into your own table.

    CREATE DATABASE learn_db;
USE WAREHOUSE learn_wh;
CREATE TABLE learn_db.public.customer_copy AS
SELECT * FROM snowflake_sample_data.tpch_sf1.customer;

    Expected: Table CUSTOMER_COPY successfully created. The copy needed compute, so LEARN_WH started by itself.

  3. Send the librarians home on purpose, then check they have gone.

    ALTER WAREHOUSE learn_wh SUSPEND;
SHOW WAREHOUSES LIKE 'LEARN_WH';

    Expected: one row where the state column says SUSPENDED. If the first line complains that the warehouse cannot be suspended, it had already gone on break after 60 seconds; that is fine, carry on.

  4. With compute switched off, ask the front-desk manager about the shelves. This reads the catalogue only, so it needs no warehouse.

    SHOW TABLES IN learn_db.public;

    Expected: one row for CUSTOMER_COPY with rows = 150000 and a bytes value of a few megabytes. The data is safely on the shelves while the warehouse is asleep. Run SHOW WAREHOUSES LIKE 'LEARN_WH'; again to confirm it is still SUSPENDED.

  5. Now ask a real question. This needs work, so the librarians come back automatically (auto-resume).

    SELECT c_mktsegment AS segment, AVG(c_acctbal) AS avg_balance
FROM learn_db.public.customer_copy
GROUP BY c_mktsegment
ORDER BY avg_balance DESC;

SHOW WAREHOUSES LIKE 'LEARN_WH';

    Expected: 5 segments with an average balance around 4,500, then a row where state is now STARTED. You never typed a resume command.

  6. Tidy up. Keep learn_wh and learn_db for later lessons, but drop the copied table so you are not renting shelf space for it, and send the librarians home.

    DROP TABLE learn_db.public.customer_copy;
ALTER WAREHOUSE learn_wh SUSPEND;

    Expected: CUSTOMER_COPY successfully dropped. and Statement executed successfully.

Key takeaway

Remember

Shelves hold the books, librarians find the answers, and the front desk keeps the catalogue. You rent shelves by the terabyte and pay librarians by the second, and switching the librarians off never touches the books.

Glossary

Storage
The layer that holds your tables in cloud disks. Billed per terabyte per month.
Compute
The processing power that runs queries. In Snowflake this means virtual warehouses.
Virtual warehouse
A named team of compute that runs your SQL. Lesson 1.3 covers sizes and settings.
Cloud services
The layer that logs you in, plans queries and keeps metadata. Mostly free.
Metadata
Facts about your data, such as table names, row counts and sizes, kept by cloud services.
Micro-partition
A small compressed chunk of a table. Snowflake splits every table into these automatically.
Result cache
A saved answer to a recent query, returned instantly if you ask the exact same question again.
Suspend / resume
Switching a warehouse off and on. Data is never affected.
