Virtual warehouses

A virtual warehouse is the compute that runs your SQL. Learn the sizes, the on/off switches, and when to make one bigger versus adding more of them.

15 min Beginner Practice worksheet + 3 challenges 1 lab

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Explain what a virtual warehouse is and is not
  • List the sizes from X-Small to 6X-Large and what changes between them
  • Set auto-suspend and auto-resume and say why they save money
  • Choose between scaling up and scaling out for a given problem

Definition

Key definition

A virtual warehouse is a named bundle of compute power that runs your queries. It has a size, it can be switched on and off, and it never shares its power with another warehouse.

The name confuses everyone at first. A Snowflake warehouse holds no data. It is only the engine. The data lives in storage (Lesson 1.2), and any warehouse can read any table it has permission to see.

  • You give it a name, such as FINANCE_WH.
  • You pick a size, from X-Small to 6X-Large.
  • It is either started (costing credits) or suspended (costing nothing).
  • Every query you run uses exactly one warehouse, the one your session has selected with USE WAREHOUSE.

Think of it like a team of librarians

In the library in the sky a warehouse is one team of librarians. A small team is fine for quick questions. For a huge question, such as counting every word in every book, you want a bigger team, because they split the shelves between them and finish sooner. When nobody asks anything, the team goes on break and stops being paid. The moment a question arrives, they come back. At rush hour, when a queue forms at the desk, the library can call in a second identical team so people stop waiting.

In the libraryTeam sizeIn Snowflake: warehouse size. Each size up doubles the team
In the libraryGoing on breakIn Snowflake: auto-suspend after N seconds idle; auto-resume when a query arrives
In the libraryExtra teams at rush hourIn Snowflake: multi-cluster warehouses that add clusters when queries queue

Sizes: from XS to 6XL

Every step up doubles the compute and doubles the credits per hour. A bigger team finishes a big job in about half the time, so the cost of that one job stays roughly the same. What you buy with a bigger size is speed, not a discount.

Good to know

Bigger is not always faster. A tiny query on an X-Large warehouse takes about as long as on an X-Small, because there is not enough work to share out. Snowflake’s own advice is to try the same query on two sizes and measure, rather than guess.

The two switches: auto-suspend and auto-resume

Auto-suspendHow many seconds of quiet before the team goes on break. Default is 600 (10 minutes). Set it to 0 or NULL and the warehouse never sleeps, which can be very expensive.AUTO_SUSPEND = 60
Auto-resumeWhether the team comes back on its own when a query arrives. Default is TRUE. If FALSE, someone must run ALTER WAREHOUSE ... RESUME first.AUTO_RESUME = TRUE

Two details matter for cost. First, every time a warehouse starts you pay for at least 60 seconds, even if the query took two. So a suspend time that is too short makes a busy warehouse start and stop all day, and each restart costs a minute. Second, when a warehouse suspends it forgets the data it had cached on its local disks, so the first queries after a resume can be a little slower. For a team that queries all day, 5 to 10 minutes is a sensible suspend time. For learning, 60 seconds is right.

Scaling up vs scaling out

There are two different problems, and each has its own fix. Mixing them up is the most common mistake on real projects.

Scale up (resize)Scale out (multi-cluster)
The problemOne query is too slowToo many people at once; queries queue
The fixMake the team bigger: Small to MediumAdd identical teams: 1 cluster to 3 clusters
LibraryMore librarians on one questionMore teams at the desk during rush hour
SQLSET WAREHOUSE_SIZE = MEDIUMSET MAX_CLUSTER_COUNT = 3
Who decidesYou, by handSnowflake, automatically, between MIN and MAX
EditionAll editionsEnterprise edition or higher

A multi-cluster warehouse has a minimum and a maximum number of clusters. When they are equal, every cluster runs all the time (maximized mode). When the minimum is lower, Snowflake starts extra clusters as queues build and stops them when the rush is over (auto-scale mode). The scaling policy sets its mood: STANDARD starts a new cluster as soon as queries queue; ECONOMY waits until it believes the new cluster will stay busy for at least 6 minutes, saving credits at the price of some waiting.

In practice: a bank

A global bank gives each team its own warehouse. This is the golden rule: one warehouse per team or workload. It stops one team’s heavy work slowing another, and it makes the bill easy to read, because every team’s cost sits under its own name.

WarehouseSizeClustersAuto-suspendWhy
FRAUD_WHSmall1 to 360 sThousands of tiny checks; rush hours need more teams, not bigger ones
RISK_WHX-Large110 minOne giant month-end model; needs one big team
REPORTING_WHLarge1 to 42 minHundreds of managers open dashboards at 9 am
MARKETING_WHX-Small160 sRare, small campaign lists

Notice how the Fraud team never scaled up. Their queries are small and fast; the trouble was queueing at 9 am and 6 pm. Extra clusters fixed that. The Risk team never scaled out. They have one user running one enormous query, and only a bigger team makes it finish sooner.

The SQL you will see

-- create a team; INITIALLY_SUSPENDED keeps it off until the first query
CREATE WAREHOUSE fraud_wh WITH
  WAREHOUSE_SIZE = SMALL
  AUTO_SUSPEND = 60
  AUTO_RESUME = TRUE
  INITIALLY_SUSPENDED = TRUE;

-- scale up: one slow query
ALTER WAREHOUSE fraud_wh SET WAREHOUSE_SIZE = MEDIUM;

-- scale out: queues at rush hour (Enterprise edition)
ALTER WAREHOUSE fraud_wh SET
  MIN_CLUSTER_COUNT = 1 MAX_CLUSTER_COUNT = 3 SCALING_POLICY = STANDARD;

-- the switches, by hand
ALTER WAREHOUSE fraud_wh SUSPEND;
ALTER WAREHOUSE fraud_wh RESUME;

-- pick a team for this session, then look at all teams
USE WAREHOUSE fraud_wh;
SHOW WAREHOUSES;

SHOW WAREHOUSES returns one row per warehouse with columns such as state (STARTED or SUSPENDED), size, auto_suspend, auto_resume, min_cluster_count and max_cluster_count. Get used to reading it; it is the first thing to check when someone says “Snowflake is slow today”.

Lab: resize a warehouse and watch it change

Scale up, scale out, and put it all back

About 15 minUses your free trial

  1. Open a SQL worksheet. Make sure you have the learning warehouse from Lesson 1.2; this creates it only if it is missing, starting suspended.

    CREATE WAREHOUSE IF NOT EXISTS learn_wh WITH WAREHOUSE_SIZE = XSMALL
  AUTO_SUSPEND = 60 AUTO_RESUME = TRUE INITIALLY_SUSPENDED = TRUE;
SHOW WAREHOUSES LIKE 'LEARN_WH';

    Expected: one row with size = X-Small, auto_suspend = 60, auto_resume = true and state = SUSPENDED.

  2. Turn off the result cache for this session, otherwise the second run below returns the saved answer instantly and you learn nothing. Then run a real question on 15 million sample orders and note the time shown next to the results.

    ALTER SESSION SET USE_CACHED_RESULT = FALSE;
USE WAREHOUSE learn_wh;

SELECT o_orderpriority AS priority, COUNT(*) AS orders, SUM(o_totalprice) AS total
FROM snowflake_sample_data.tpch_sf10.orders
GROUP BY o_orderpriority
ORDER BY o_orderpriority;

    Expected: 5 rows (1-URGENT to 5-LOW), each with about 3 million orders. Write down the seconds it took; the first run includes the warehouse waking up.

  3. Scale up. Make the team twice as big, confirm the new size, then run exactly the same query again.

    ALTER WAREHOUSE learn_wh SET WAREHOUSE_SIZE = SMALL;
SHOW WAREHOUSES LIKE 'LEARN_WH';

    Expected: size now reads Small. Rerun the query from step 2. It usually finishes faster, though on data this small the gap can be modest. The point is that you resized a running engine with one line and no downtime.

  4. Scale out. Trials run on Enterprise edition, so you can allow a second cluster for rush hours.

    ALTER WAREHOUSE learn_wh SET
  MIN_CLUSTER_COUNT = 1 MAX_CLUSTER_COUNT = 2 SCALING_POLICY = ECONOMY;
SHOW WAREHOUSES LIKE 'LEARN_WH';

    Expected: min_cluster_count = 1, max_cluster_count = 2, scaling_policy = ECONOMY. The second cluster only starts if queries queue, so this costs nothing right now. If you see an error about the edition, your account is Standard; note that and move on.

  5. Look at your own query history to see which size ran each query. Times are in milliseconds.

    SELECT start_time, warehouse_name, warehouse_size, total_elapsed_time / 1000 AS seconds
FROM TABLE(information_schema.query_history(result_limit => 20))
WHERE query_text LIKE '%tpch_sf10.orders%'
ORDER BY start_time DESC;

    Expected: two rows for your orders query, one with warehouse_size X-Small and one Small, each with its seconds. Compare them.

  6. Put everything back so the trial stays cheap: X-Small, one cluster, result cache on, and the team on break.

    ALTER WAREHOUSE learn_wh SET WAREHOUSE_SIZE = XSMALL MAX_CLUSTER_COUNT = 1;
ALTER SESSION SET USE_CACHED_RESULT = TRUE;
ALTER WAREHOUSE learn_wh SUSPEND;
SHOW WAREHOUSES LIKE 'LEARN_WH';

    Expected: size X-Small, max_cluster_count 1, state SUSPENDED. If SUSPEND says the warehouse is already suspended, it went on break by itself; that is fine.

Key takeaway

Remember

A warehouse is a team of librarians, not a room of books. Size up when one query is slow, scale out when many people are queueing, let it sleep when idle, and give every team its own.

Glossary

Virtual warehouse
A named, sized bundle of compute that runs SQL. Holds no data.
Warehouse size
X-Small to 6X-Large. Each step doubles compute and credits per hour.
Auto-suspend
Seconds of inactivity before the warehouse switches itself off. Default 600.
Auto-resume
Whether a query automatically switches the warehouse back on. Default TRUE.
Scale up
Resizing one warehouse to a bigger size. Fixes slow single queries.
Scale out
Adding clusters to a multi-cluster warehouse. Fixes queueing.
Multi-cluster warehouse
A warehouse with MIN and MAX cluster counts. Enterprise edition or higher.
Scaling policy
STANDARD starts clusters quickly; ECONOMY waits to save credits.
Queueing
Queries waiting because the warehouse is already full. The sign you need to scale out.
