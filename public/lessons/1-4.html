Credits and cost

Snowflake charges in credits. Learn what a credit buys, how the bill is worked out to the second, and the habits that keep a bank’s bill small.

12 min Beginner Practice worksheet + 3 challenges 1 lab

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Explain what a credit is and how many each warehouse size burns per hour
  • Work out the cost of a query from its size and its seconds
  • Name the three parts of a Snowflake bill: compute, storage and cloud services
  • Set up a resource monitor so nobody can run up a surprise bill

Definition

Key definition

A credit is Snowflake’s unit of work. A warehouse burns credits only while it is running, at a rate fixed by its size. The company buys credits in its own currency, so credits times price is the compute bill.

A Snowflake bill has three parts, and it helps to keep them apart in your head:

  • Compute: credits burned by virtual warehouses. Almost always the biggest part.
  • Storage: a flat price per terabyte per month for what sits on the shelves. Billed separately from credits.
  • Cloud services: the front-desk manager. Free unless it goes above 10% of the day’s warehouse credits, which is rare.

Think of it like librarian wages

Librarians in the sky library are paid by the second, but only while they are working. A team of two costs twice a team of one. The moment a team starts a shift you owe at least one minute of wages, even if the question took ten seconds. Shelf rent is a different bill, paid monthly per metre of shelf you fill, whether anyone reads the books or not. The front-desk manager is included in the price unless you keep them unusually busy.

In the libraryWages by the secondIn Snowflake: credits per second while a warehouse runs, 60-second minimum per start
In the libraryShelf rentIn Snowflake: storage, a flat price per TB per month, separate from credits
In the librarySpending capIn Snowflake: a resource monitor that warns or suspends at a credit limit

The billing rules

Per secondA running warehouse is billed for every second it is on, not rounded up to the hour.Rule 1
60-second minimumEach time a warehouse starts (or resumes) you pay for at least one minute. A 5-second query on a fresh warehouse costs 60 seconds.Rule 2
Idle time countsA started warehouse with no queries still burns credits until auto-suspend kicks in. Idle is the silent cost.Rule 3
Clusters multiplyA Medium warehouse running 3 clusters for an hour costs 4 x 3 = 12 credits.Rule 4
Resizing up bills the differenceGoing from Small to Medium bills a one-minute minimum for the two extra credits per hour, not the whole warehouse again.Rule 5
Storage is separateCompressed size, averaged daily, charged per TB per month. Time Travel and Fail-safe copies count too.Rule 6
Worked example

A Large warehouse (8 credits an hour) runs a report for 15 minutes, then sits idle for 10 minutes before auto-suspend. Billed time is 25 minutes: 8 x 25 / 60 = 3.33 credits. The idle 10 minutes cost 1.33 credits, forty percent of the total, for doing nothing. That is why auto-suspend matters.

Resource monitors: the spending cap

A resource monitor watches credits burned by one or more warehouses (or the whole account) over a period such as a month, and acts when a percentage of the quota is reached.

TriggerWhat happensLibrary
NOTIFYEmail the account admins; nothing stopsA note on the manager’s desk: “80% of the wage budget is spent”
SUSPENDLet running queries finish, then suspend the warehousesFinish the questions in hand, then send everyone home
SUSPEND_IMMEDIATECancel running queries and suspend nowDown tools this second

Only ACCOUNTADMIN can create monitors. Thresholds can go above 100%, and one monitor may have several notify triggers but only one SUSPEND and one SUSPEND_IMMEDIATE. An account has at most one account-level monitor; each warehouse can be assigned to one warehouse-level monitor.

Cost-saving habits

Most Snowflake waste is boring and easy to prevent. These are the habits good teams follow from day one.

  • Short auto-suspend. 60 seconds for learning and ad hoc work, 5 to 10 minutes for busy shared warehouses. Never leave it at 0.
  • Start small. Create warehouses as X-Small and size up only when you measure that a query is too slow.
  • One warehouse per team. The bill then reads like a list of teams, and nobody hides inside someone else’s budget.
  • Do not run SELECT * on huge tables. Ask for the columns and rows you need; less work means fewer credits.
  • Reuse results. The same query on unchanged data comes from the result cache and costs no warehouse time.
  • Monitor everything. A resource monitor on every warehouse, with a notify at 80% and suspend at 100%.
  • Look at the bill weekly. Snowsight has an Admin → Cost Management page (the exact menu name varies a little by account) that shows credits by warehouse and by day.

A global bank reviews last week’s credits by warehouse. One line stands out.

One setting cost more than every other team combined. The fix took one line: ALTER WAREHOUSE adhoc_wh SET AUTO_SUSPEND = 300; and a resource monitor so it can never happen again. This is the kind of thing you will be asked to check in your first weeks, and now you can.

The SQL you will see

-- a spending cap: warn at 80%, stop at 100% of 100 credits a month
USE ROLE ACCOUNTADMIN;
CREATE RESOURCE MONITOR team_cap WITH CREDIT_QUOTA = 100
  FREQUENCY = MONTHLY START_TIMESTAMP = IMMEDIATELY
  TRIGGERS ON 80 PERCENT DO NOTIFY
           ON 100 PERCENT DO SUSPEND;
ALTER WAREHOUSE adhoc_wh SET RESOURCE_MONITOR = team_cap;

-- last 7 days of credits by warehouse (data can lag by up to 3 hours)
SELECT warehouse_name, SUM(credits_used) AS credits
FROM snowflake.account_usage.warehouse_metering_history
WHERE start_time >= DATEADD('day', -7, CURRENT_TIMESTAMP())
GROUP BY warehouse_name
ORDER BY credits DESC;

-- the cheapest fix in Snowflake
ALTER WAREHOUSE adhoc_wh SET AUTO_SUSPEND = 300;

Lab: see your credits and cap them

Read the meter, then install a spending cap

About 15 minUses your free trial

  1. Open a SQL worksheet and check that your learning warehouse still has a short auto-suspend. If you skipped earlier labs, create it first with the command from Lesson 1.3.

    SHOW WAREHOUSES;

    Expected: at least two rows, COMPUTE_WH and LEARN_WH. Read the size and auto_suspend columns. LEARN_WH should say X-Small and 60. Trial accounts usually create COMPUTE_WH with a 600-second suspend; that is fine for now.

  2. Burn a few known seconds of credits, then look at what each query cost in time. Elapsed time is in milliseconds, so divide by 1000.

    USE WAREHOUSE learn_wh;
SELECT COUNT(*) AS line_items, SUM(l_extendedprice) AS total
FROM snowflake_sample_data.tpch_sf10.lineitem;

SELECT start_time, warehouse_name, warehouse_size,
       total_elapsed_time / 1000 AS seconds,
       total_elapsed_time / 1000 / 3600 AS credits_if_xsmall
FROM TABLE(information_schema.query_history(result_limit => 5))
ORDER BY start_time DESC;

    Expected: the first query returns one row of about 60 million line items. The second lists your last 5 queries with seconds and a tiny credit estimate (an X-Small burns 1 credit per 3,600 seconds). Remember the 60-second minimum if the warehouse had just woken up.

  3. Read the real meter. This view is the official record of credits by warehouse and hour. It can lag by up to 3 hours, so if it is empty for today, run it again later in the lab or tomorrow.

    SELECT warehouse_name, start_time, credits_used
FROM snowflake.account_usage.warehouse_metering_history
WHERE start_time >= DATEADD('day', -7, CURRENT_TIMESTAMP())
ORDER BY start_time DESC;

    Expected: one row per warehouse per hour with a small credits_used value, for example 0.02 for a minute of X-Small. In Snowsight you can see the same numbers as a chart under Admin → Cost Management (menu names vary slightly by account).

  4. Install a spending cap on the learning warehouse: 5 credits a month, warn at 80%, suspend at 100%. This needs the ACCOUNTADMIN role, which a trial gives you.

    USE ROLE ACCOUNTADMIN;
CREATE RESOURCE MONITOR learn_cap WITH CREDIT_QUOTA = 5
  FREQUENCY = MONTHLY START_TIMESTAMP = IMMEDIATELY
  TRIGGERS ON 80 PERCENT DO NOTIFY
           ON 100 PERCENT DO SUSPEND;
ALTER WAREHOUSE learn_wh SET RESOURCE_MONITOR = learn_cap;
SHOW RESOURCE MONITORS;

    Expected: Resource monitor LEARN_CAP successfully created., then one row for LEARN_CAP with credit_quota 5, frequency MONTHLY and level WAREHOUSE. If you see it listed with used_credits a small fraction, the meter is already counting.

  5. Apply the cheapest habit to the trial’s default warehouse too, then send the librarians home. Keep the monitor; it costs nothing and protects your free credits for the rest of the course.

    ALTER WAREHOUSE compute_wh SET AUTO_SUSPEND = 60;
ALTER WAREHOUSE learn_wh SUSPEND;
SHOW WAREHOUSES;

    Expected: both warehouses show auto_suspend = 60 and LEARN_WH shows state SUSPENDED. If SUSPEND says it is already suspended, auto-suspend beat you to it.

Key takeaway

Remember

Credits are librarian wages: paid by the second, one-minute minimum per shift, doubled with every team size. Shelf rent is a separate monthly bill. Short auto-suspend, small sizes and a resource monitor stop almost every surprise.

Glossary

Credit
Snowflake’s unit of compute work. Burned by running warehouses at a rate set by size.
Per-second billing
Credits are charged for each second a warehouse runs, after a 60-second minimum per start.
Idle time
Seconds a warehouse is started but running nothing. Still billed until auto-suspend.
Storage cost
A flat price per terabyte per month for compressed data, including Time Travel and Fail-safe copies.
Cloud services cost
Credits for the front-desk layer. Only charged for the part above 10% of daily warehouse credits.
Resource monitor
A credit quota with triggers that notify or suspend warehouses. Created by ACCOUNTADMIN.
Credit quota
The number of credits a resource monitor allows per period (daily, weekly, monthly, yearly or never resetting).
WAREHOUSE_METERING_HISTORY
The account usage view that records credits per warehouse per hour for the last year.
