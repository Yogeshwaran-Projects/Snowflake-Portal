Secure data sharing

Let another team or another company read your data live, without sending a single file. Learn what a share is, who the provider and consumer are, how reader accounts and the Marketplace work, and how secure views let you share only part of a table.

25 min Beginner Practice worksheet + 3 challenges 1 lab

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Explain a share, a provider and a consumer in one breath
  • Say why shared data is never copied and always current
  • Create a share and add a table or secure view to it with SQL
  • Know when to use a reader account and what the Marketplace is for

Definition

Key definition

Secure data sharing lets one Snowflake account (the provider) give another account (the consumer) live, read-only access to chosen tables and views, with no data copied or moved.

The old way of sharing data was to export a file, email or upload it, and let the other side load it. By the time they looked at it, it was already out of date, and now two copies existed that could drift apart. A share fixes both problems: there is only ever one copy, the provider's, and the consumer always sees the current version.

  • Share: a named object in the provider account that lists what is shared and with whom.
  • Provider: the account that owns the data and creates the share.
  • Consumer: the account that reads the shared data. It can query, but never change it.

Think of it like a glass reading window

A friend from another library wants to read your bank ledger shelf. You do not photocopy it and post it. Instead you put a glass window in the wall. Your friend reads your shelf through the glass whenever she likes. She always sees the shelf as it is right now, because it is the real shelf. She cannot reach through the glass to write in the books, and she cannot take any away. If you want, you frost part of the glass so she sees only some shelves, or only some columns of each index card.

In the libraryGlass windowIn Snowflake: a share. Live, read-only, nothing copied.
In the libraryYour friend without a library cardIn Snowflake: a reader account. You create it for her, and you pay for her reading time.
In the libraryFrosted glassIn Snowflake: a secure view. Share only chosen rows and columns.

The librarians who fetch the books for your friend are her own team, not yours. In Snowflake terms: the consumer uses its own virtual warehouse and pays for that compute. The provider pays only for storing the data, exactly as before.

In practice: a bank

A global bank holds card transactions for millions of customers. Several outside parties need some of that data, and none of them may see all of it.

WhoWhat they needHow the bank shares it
Fraud analytics partner (has Snowflake)Transactions, but no names or card numbersSecure view that hides those columns; added to a share; partner's account added as consumer
Regional audit firm (no Snowflake)Only one region's transactionsSecure view filtered by region; shared to a reader account the bank creates
RegulatorA monthly summarySecure view over an aggregated table; the regulator's own account as consumer
The bank itselfExchange rates from a data vendorGets the vendor's listing from the Marketplace; no files to load

Nothing is exported, so nothing can be lost in transit. When the audit ends, the bank removes the reader account and access stops that second.

The SQL you will use

The provider side is four steps: make a secure view if you need to hide something, create the share, grant it access, and add the consumer's account. Creating a share needs the ACCOUNTADMIN role by default.

-- 1. Frost the glass: a secure view with only the rows and columns to share
CREATE SECURE VIEW ledger_db.public.mumbai_txns_v AS
  SELECT txn_id, merchant_type, amount, txn_date
  FROM ledger_db.public.card_transactions
  WHERE city = 'Mumbai';

-- 2. Create the share (the window)
CREATE SHARE mumbai_share;

-- 3. Say what can be seen through it: database, schema, then the objects
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE ledger_db TO SHARE mumbai_share;
GRANT USAGE ON SCHEMA ledger_db.public TO SHARE mumbai_share;
GRANT SELECT ON VIEW ledger_db.public.mumbai_txns_v TO SHARE mumbai_share;

-- 4. Say who may look through it (organisation name and account name of the consumer)
ALTER SHARE mumbai_share ADD ACCOUNTS = myorg.partner_account;

-- Check your work
SHOW SHARES;
DESC SHARE mumbai_share;
SHOW GRANTS TO SHARE mumbai_share;

On the consumer side, the shared data is turned into a database and then queried like any other. For a partner with no Snowflake account, the provider creates a reader account instead and adds it to the share.

-- Consumer: turn the share into a database (run in the consumer account)
CREATE DATABASE bank_mumbai FROM SHARE myorg.bank_account.mumbai_share;
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM bank_mumbai.public.mumbai_txns_v;

-- Provider: a reader account for a partner without Snowflake (provider pays its compute)
CREATE MANAGED ACCOUNT audit_reader
  ADMIN_NAME = audit_admin, ADMIN_PASSWORD = 'choose-a-strong-password', TYPE = READER;

Lab: build a share and browse the Marketplace

Secure data sharing in your trial account

About 15 minFree trial (Enterprise edition)

  1. Open a SQL worksheet as ACCOUNTADMIN, point it at the learning database and build the table to share.

    USE ROLE ACCOUNTADMIN;
CREATE WAREHOUSE IF NOT EXISTS learn_wh WITH WAREHOUSE_SIZE = XSMALL AUTO_SUSPEND = 60 AUTO_RESUME = TRUE;
CREATE DATABASE IF NOT EXISTS learn_db;
USE WAREHOUSE learn_wh;
USE SCHEMA learn_db.public;

CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE accounts (account_no NUMBER, customer_name VARCHAR, city VARCHAR, balance NUMBER);
INSERT INTO accounts VALUES
  (1001, 'Asha Rao', 'Mumbai', 52000),
  (1002, 'Vikram Shah', 'Delhi', 210000),
  (1003, 'Meera Iyer', 'Chennai', 34500),
  (1004, 'Divya Kumar', 'Mumbai', 76000),
  (1005, 'Arjun Das', 'Delhi', 61000);

    Expected: “5 rows inserted”.

  2. Frost the glass. Make a secure view that shows Mumbai accounts only and hides customer names. Make a normal view too, so you can see the difference in a moment.

    CREATE OR REPLACE SECURE VIEW accounts_mumbai_v AS
  SELECT account_no, city, balance FROM accounts WHERE city = 'Mumbai';

CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW accounts_plain_v AS
  SELECT account_no, city, balance FROM accounts;

SELECT * FROM accounts_mumbai_v;

    Expected: 2 rows (1001 and 1004), three columns, no names.

  3. Create the share and grant it the database, the schema and the secure view.

    CREATE SHARE learn_share;
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE learn_db TO SHARE learn_share;
GRANT USAGE ON SCHEMA learn_db.public TO SHARE learn_share;
GRANT SELECT ON VIEW learn_db.public.accounts_mumbai_v TO SHARE learn_share;

    Expected: “Share LEARN_SHARE successfully created.” and “Statement executed successfully.” three times.

  4. Now try to share the normal view. Snowflake refuses.

    GRANT SELECT ON VIEW learn_db.public.accounts_plain_v TO SHARE learn_share;

    Expected: an error saying only secure views can be shared. That rule protects the provider: a normal view could reveal the tables and logic behind it.

  5. Inspect the share.

    SHOW SHARES;
DESC SHARE learn_share;
SHOW GRANTS TO SHARE learn_share;

    Expected: SHOW SHARES lists LEARN_SHARE with kind OUTBOUND and an empty to-column (no consumers yet). You should also see an INBOUND share, the one that brings SNOWFLAKE_SAMPLE_DATA into your trial. DESC SHARE lists the database, schema and view with their kinds.

  6. Adding a consumer is optional, because it needs a second Snowflake account in the same cloud region. If a colleague has one, ask for their organisation and account name and run the ALTER SHARE below; they can then run the CREATE DATABASE … FROM SHARE from the SQL section. If not, just read the command and move on.

    -- Only if you have a second account: replace with their org and account name
ALTER SHARE learn_share ADD ACCOUNTS = their_org.their_account;

    Expected (if run): “Statement executed successfully.” and the account now appears in the to-column of SHOW SHARES.

  7. Browse the Marketplace. In the Snowsight left menu open Data Products → Marketplace (the menu name can vary slightly between account versions). Search for a free listing, for example weather or currency data, and open it. Read the description and the sample queries. You can select Get to add it as a database to your account; it costs nothing to add a free listing, and querying it uses your own warehouse.

    Expected: the listing page shows the provider, the tables included and example SQL. If you get it, a new database appears under Data → Databases.

  8. Clean up. Keep learn_db and learn_wh for the next lesson.

    DROP SHARE learn_share;
DROP VIEW accounts_plain_v;
DROP VIEW accounts_mumbai_v;
DROP TABLE accounts;

    Expected: “successfully dropped” four times. Dropping a share removes all consumers' access to it at once.

Key takeaway

Remember

A share is a glass window: the consumer reads your live data, nothing is copied, and they can never write. Frost the glass with a secure view to share only some rows or columns, use a reader account for partners without Snowflake, and shop the Marketplace instead of loading files.

Glossary

Share
A named object in the provider account listing the database objects shared and the accounts allowed to read them.
Provider
The account that owns the data and creates the share.
Consumer
The account that reads shared data. Read-only, using its own compute.
Reader account
A Snowflake account the provider creates and pays for, so a partner without Snowflake can query a share.
Secure view
A view whose definition is hidden and which can be shared. Used to expose only chosen rows and columns.
Listing
A share packaged with a description, published privately or on the Marketplace.
Snowflake Marketplace
The public catalogue of data listings you can add to your account.
Inbound / outbound
A share you receive is inbound; one you create for others is outbound.
