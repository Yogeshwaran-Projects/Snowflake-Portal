Roles and access control

Learn how Snowflake decides who may see and change what: roles, privileges, grants, and the extra locks a bank puts on card numbers and account numbers.

30 min Beginner Practice worksheet + 3 challenges 1 lab

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Explain how privileges, roles and users fit together
  • Name the six system roles and say what each is for
  • Create a custom role and grant it exactly what it needs
  • Use SHOW GRANTS to answer “who can read this table?”

Definition

Key definition

Access control in Snowflake is role-based (RBAC): privileges are granted to roles, and roles are granted to users. A user can only do what one of their roles allows.

Notice what is not in that sentence. You never grant a privilege straight to a person. Everything goes through a role. That is the one rule that makes the rest of this lesson simple.

  • Privilege: permission to do one thing on one object, such as SELECT on a table or USAGE on a warehouse.
  • Role: a named bundle of privileges, such as analyst_role.
  • User: a login. A user holds one or more roles and switches between them.
  • Role hierarchy: a role can be granted to another role. The senior role then gets everything the junior role has.

Think of it like ID badges in the library

Nobody walks around the sky library with a bunch of keys. Everyone wears an ID badge. The badge decides which rooms you can enter and which shelves you can take books from. The security office decides what each badge opens; it does not decide anything about you personally. If you move teams, you hand back one badge and receive another.

In the libraryID badgeIn Snowflake: a role. Users wear one badge at a time (USE ROLE) but may own several.
In the libraryWhich doors the badge opensIn Snowflake: privileges and grants. USAGE opens a wing or room, SELECT lets you read a shelf.
In the librarySenior badge includes junior badgesIn Snowflake: role hierarchy. Grant analyst_role to sysadmin and sysadmin can do everything an analyst can.

And the master key that opens everything? That is ACCOUNTADMIN. It stays in a safe, is held by two or three people, and is never used for daily work.

The system roles

Every account comes with these roles already made. They form a ladder: each one is granted to the one above it, so the top role inherits everything below.

RoleWhat it is forIn the library
ORGADMINManages the organisation: creates accounts, sees usage across all of them. Not part of the ladder.Head office that owns several libraries
ACCOUNTADMINThe top role in one account. Inherits SYSADMIN and SECURITYADMIN. Billing, account settings, emergencies.The master key in the safe
SECURITYADMINManages grants on any object account-wide. Inherits USERADMIN.Head of the security office
USERADMINCreates and manages users and roles, nothing else.The badge-printing desk
SYSADMINCreates warehouses, databases, schemas and tables. Custom roles should be granted up to it.Head librarian who builds wings and shelves

Good to know

Your trial account gives you ACCOUNTADMIN, which is why the labs work. In a bank you will almost never hold it. Expect a custom role such as project_dev_role and a ticket process to get more.

How grants work

To read one table, a role needs a chain of small permissions, one per level of the building. Miss one and the query fails with “does not exist or not authorized”.

ObjectPrivilege neededMeaning
WarehouseUSAGEAllowed to hire this team of librarians
DatabaseUSAGEAllowed to enter the wing
SchemaUSAGEAllowed to enter the room
TableSELECTAllowed to read the shelf. INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE are separate privileges.
OwnershipWhichever role creates an object owns it and can do anything to it, including granting it to others. Ownership can be handed over with GRANT OWNERSHIP.One owner per object
Least privilegeGive each role the smallest set of privileges that lets the job get done. A reporting role gets SELECT, never DELETE.The bank’s golden rule
Custom rolesMade with CREATE ROLE by USERADMIN or above. Name them by job: analyst_role, fraud_role, data_engineer_role.Grant them to SYSADMIN
Checking accessSHOW GRANTS TO ROLE, SHOW GRANTS ON TABLE and SHOW GRANTS TO USER answer almost every “why can’t I see this?” question.Your first debugging tool

In practice: a bank

A global bank keeps card numbers (PAN), account numbers and customer names in Snowflake. The regulator requires that only people who need a full card number ever see one. Roles alone are not enough for that, because the fraud team and the marketing team query the same table. Two Enterprise Edition features finish the job:

FeatureWhat it doesBank example
Masking policyHides or scrambles a column depending on the role running the query.fraud_role sees 4532 0151 1283 0366; everyone else sees XXXX XXXX XXXX 0366.
Row access policyHides whole rows depending on the role.The South region team only gets rows where region = 'SOUTH'.

The policy is attached to the table once. Every query, from every tool, obeys it automatically. Nobody has to remember to filter.

The SQL you will use

Create a role, give it the chain of privileges, hand it to a person, then check your work.

-- 1. Make the badge (USERADMIN or higher)
CREATE ROLE analyst_role;

-- 2. Decide which doors it opens
GRANT USAGE ON WAREHOUSE learn_wh TO ROLE analyst_role;
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE learn_db TO ROLE analyst_role;
GRANT USAGE ON SCHEMA learn_db.public TO ROLE analyst_role;
GRANT SELECT ON TABLE learn_db.public.accounts TO ROLE analyst_role;

-- 3. Give the badge to a person, and roll it up to SYSADMIN
GRANT ROLE analyst_role TO USER asha;
GRANT ROLE analyst_role TO ROLE sysadmin;

-- 4. Put the badge on and work
USE ROLE analyst_role;

-- 5. Take a door away again
REVOKE SELECT ON TABLE learn_db.public.accounts FROM ROLE analyst_role;

-- 6. Who can do what?
SHOW GRANTS TO ROLE analyst_role;
SHOW GRANTS ON TABLE learn_db.public.accounts;
SHOW GRANTS TO USER asha;

Masking and row access policies look like small functions that return the value (or TRUE/FALSE) for the current role. You attach them to a column or table once.

-- Show the full card number only to fraud_role; everyone else sees the last 4 digits
CREATE MASKING POLICY mask_pan AS (val VARCHAR) RETURNS VARCHAR ->
  CASE WHEN CURRENT_ROLE() IN ('FRAUD_ROLE') THEN val
       ELSE 'XXXXXXXXXXXX' || RIGHT(val, 4) END;
ALTER TABLE cards MODIFY COLUMN pan SET MASKING POLICY mask_pan;

-- Show South region rows only to the South team (ACCOUNTADMIN sees all)
CREATE ROW ACCESS POLICY region_rap AS (region VARCHAR) RETURNS BOOLEAN ->
  CURRENT_ROLE() = 'ACCOUNTADMIN' OR (CURRENT_ROLE() = 'SOUTH_ROLE' AND region = 'SOUTH');
ALTER TABLE accounts ADD ROW ACCESS POLICY region_rap ON (region);

Practice worksheet

This worksheet simulates the grants inside a small bank. users_roles says which badge each person holds and role_grants says which doors each badge opens. Join them to answer “who can read what?”. Open the Schema tab to see the columns.

Lab: build a read-only role and prove it is read-only

Create analyst_role, grant it, switch to it, watch a write fail

About 20 minTrial account, ACCOUNTADMIN

  1. Open a SQL worksheet in Snowsight. Make sure you have the practice database, a small warehouse and a table to protect. Run all of this as ACCOUNTADMIN:

    USE ROLE ACCOUNTADMIN;
CREATE WAREHOUSE IF NOT EXISTS learn_wh WITH WAREHOUSE_SIZE = 'XSMALL' AUTO_SUSPEND = 60 AUTO_RESUME = TRUE;
CREATE DATABASE IF NOT EXISTS learn_db;
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE learn_db.public.accounts (account_no VARCHAR, holder_name VARCHAR, balance NUMBER);
INSERT INTO learn_db.public.accounts VALUES
  ('AC1001', 'Asha Rao', 75000), ('AC1002', 'Vikram Shah', 12000), ('AC1003', 'Meera Iyer', 42000);
SELECT CURRENT_USER();

    Expected: “3 rows inserted”, then one row showing your username. Write that username down; you need it in step 3.

  2. Create the badge and decide which doors it opens. Only USAGE and SELECT, nothing that changes data.

    CREATE ROLE IF NOT EXISTS analyst_role;
GRANT USAGE ON WAREHOUSE learn_wh TO ROLE analyst_role;
GRANT USAGE ON DATABASE learn_db TO ROLE analyst_role;
GRANT USAGE ON SCHEMA learn_db.public TO ROLE analyst_role;
GRANT SELECT ON TABLE learn_db.public.accounts TO ROLE analyst_role;
GRANT ROLE analyst_role TO ROLE SYSADMIN;

    Expected: each statement returns “Statement executed successfully.”

  3. Hand the badge to yourself. Replace YOUR_USER with the username from step 1 (no quotes).

    GRANT ROLE analyst_role TO USER YOUR_USER;
SHOW GRANTS TO USER YOUR_USER;

    Expected: the SHOW output lists ACCOUNTADMIN and ANALYST_ROLE (and any others you already had) in the role column.

  4. Put the badge on and read the shelf.

    USE ROLE analyst_role;
USE WAREHOUSE learn_wh;
SELECT * FROM learn_db.public.accounts;

    Expected: 3 rows. The role selector at the top right of the worksheet now shows ANALYST_ROLE.

  5. Now prove the badge does not open the wrong doors. Both of these must fail.

    INSERT INTO learn_db.public.accounts VALUES ('AC1004', 'Rahul Nair', 30500);
DROP TABLE learn_db.public.accounts;

    Expected: “SQL access control error: Insufficient privileges to operate on table 'ACCOUNTS'”. That error is the whole point of this lesson. The table still has 3 rows.

  6. Switch back to the master key and look at the grants from both directions.

    USE ROLE ACCOUNTADMIN;
SHOW GRANTS TO ROLE analyst_role;
SHOW GRANTS ON TABLE learn_db.public.accounts;

    Expected: the first list shows 4 USAGE/SELECT rows (warehouse, database, schema, table). The second shows OWNERSHIP for ACCOUNTADMIN and SELECT for ANALYST_ROLE.

  7. Clean up. Dropping the role removes every grant it held. Keep learn_db and learn_wh; later lessons use them.

    DROP ROLE analyst_role;
DROP TABLE learn_db.public.accounts;

    Expected: “ANALYST_ROLE successfully dropped.” and “ACCOUNTS successfully dropped.”

Key takeaway

Remember

Privileges go to roles, roles go to people. Give every role the least it needs, keep ACCOUNTADMIN in the safe, and let masking and row access policies guard the sensitive columns and rows.

Glossary

RBAC
Role-based access control: permissions attach to roles, not to people.
Privilege
One permission on one object, such as SELECT on a table.
Grant
The act of giving a privilege to a role, or a role to a user or role.
Role hierarchy
A role granted to another role. The parent inherits the child’s privileges.
Ownership
The role that created an object. It can do anything to it and hand it over.
Least privilege
Give only what the job needs, nothing more.
Masking policy
Hides or scrambles a column for roles that should not see it.
Row access policy
Filters out whole rows for roles that should not see them.
PAN
Primary Account Number: the 16-digit card number. Highly sensitive.
