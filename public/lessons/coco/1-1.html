What is CoCo?

Meet the robot assistant of the sky library. Learn what CoCo is, who uses it, what it can and cannot do, how it differs from Cortex, and the one safety habit that matters most on bank data.

Definition

Key definition

Snowflake CoCo is an AI coding agent built into Snowflake. You describe a data task in plain English; CoCo looks at your account, writes the SQL or Python, can run it with your approval, explains the result and fixes errors.

CoCo launched as Cortex Code. Snowflake renamed it CoCo in June 2026. You will see both names in docs, blogs and project chats; they mean the same product. Some technical names still say "cortex": the command-line tool is called cortex, and the cost settings start with CORTEX_CODE_.

Three words in the definition matter:

  • Agent: a chatbot answers one message. An agent takes a goal, makes a plan, does several steps (look up tables, write a query, run it, read the error, try again) and comes back with a result.
  • Your account: CoCo reads the names of your databases, tables and columns, and it works under your role. It sees only what you are allowed to see.
  • With your approval: CoCo shows you changes before applying them. You stay in charge.

Think of it like a robot assistant in the library

Remember the library in the sky? Books are your data, shelves are tables, and teams of librarians (warehouses) fetch answers for wages (credits). Your ID badge (your role) decides which rooms you may enter.

Now the library gives you a robot assistant. It has memorised the whole library map. You say, "I need the number of customers who left last year, by city." The robot writes a request slip (the SQL), shows it to you, and, if you say yes, hands it to the librarians. When the answer comes back, it explains it. If a slip is rejected, it reads the reason and rewrites it.

In the libraryRobot assistantIn Snowflake: CoCo, which knows the library map (your metadata)
In the libraryRequest slipsIn Snowflake: the SQL and Python that CoCo writes for you
In the libraryYour ID badgeIn Snowflake: your role. CoCo opens only the rooms your badge opens

Two rules make the robot safe. First, it can only open rooms your badge opens. Second, it is still a robot: sometimes it picks the wrong shelf or mishears you. So you read every slip before it is handed in. That habit is the heart of this whole course.

CoCo can use several large language models. The docs list Claude, OpenAI GPT, Gemini and Grok models, plus three "Auto" choices that let Snowflake pick (Auto, Auto Intelligent and Auto Efficient). Which ones you see depends on your role and your region.

And what it cannot do:

  • It cannot go past your badge. If your role cannot read a table, CoCo cannot either. "Table not found" may simply mean "not granted to you".
  • It does not know your business rules. It does not know that your bank counts a customer as "active" only after 3 logins a month. You must tell it.
  • It is not always right. The docs say AI suggestions "can occasionally be incorrect or not match your intent". It can invent a column or pick the wrong join.
  • It is not free. CoCo is billed by tokens (the words it reads and writes), and any query it runs also uses warehouse credits.

One line to remember: Cortex AI works on the data inside your query; CoCo writes the query. You will often combine them, for example by asking CoCo to write a query that calls AI_SENTIMENT on support ticket text.

One more name

In the same June 2026 announcement, Snowflake Intelligence was renamed CoWork. CoWork is for business users who ask questions of governed data in plain English. CoCo is for people who write code. Do not mix them up in a meeting.

In practice: a bank

Picture your first week on a churn project at a global bank. You get read access to a schema with 200 tables and nobody has time to explain them. Here is how CoCo helps, and what you still own:

TaskCoCo doesYou still do
Find the customer tableSearches the catalog, lists likely tables with a one-line guess eachConfirm with the data owner which table is the "golden" one
Churn rate by segmentWrites the GROUP BY query and runs it with your approvalCheck how "churned" is defined and that no duplicate customers inflate counts
Query failsReads the error and proposes a fixRead the diff: did it fix the bug, or just delete the failing filter?
Clean-up requestWrites a DELETE if askedNever run destructive SQL on production from a chat; get a second reviewer

The safety mindset

On bank data, a wrong query can mis-state money, leak personal data or delete records. Keep these four rules from day one:

  1. Read before you run. Every slip, every time. If you cannot explain a line, ask CoCo to explain it.
  2. Check the numbers. Compare a count or total against something you trust.
  3. Destructive means two people. DELETE, UPDATE, DROP, TRUNCATE, GRANT: write, do not run, and get a reviewer.
  4. Data stays home. Do not paste customer records into prompts, and do not let CoCo copy data out of Snowflake unless the project allows it.

Lab: say hello to CoCo

Find CoCo in Snowsight and ask three safe questions

About 10 minYour Snowflake trial

  1. Sign in to Snowsight on your trial account. Look for the CoCo icon in the lower-right corner and select it. A panel opens on the right.

    Expected: a chat box. If there is no icon, or it says AI is not enabled, do not worry. Lesson 1.2 walks through setup (roles, cross-region inference and the trial credit card). Come back to this lab afterwards.

  2. Ask who you are. This shows CoCo works under your badge.

    What role and warehouse am I using right now, and which databases can this role see?

    Expected: your role (usually ACCOUNTADMIN on a trial), a warehouse such as COMPUTE_WH, and databases such as SNOWFLAKE and SNOWFLAKE_SAMPLE_DATA. It may run SHOW or SELECT CURRENT_ROLE() to find out.

  3. Ask it to explain, not to run.

    Explain in plain English what this does, but do not run it:
SELECT c_mktsegment, COUNT(*) FROM snowflake_sample_data.tpch_sf1.customer GROUP BY 1;

    Expected: it counts customers per market segment. "GROUP BY 1" means "group by the first column". No query runs.

  4. Test the safety habit. Ask for something destructive and watch what it does.

    Write, but do not run, a statement that drops the database SNOWFLAKE_SAMPLE_DATA.
Then tell me what would happen if I ran it.

    Expected: CoCo writes the DROP statement without running it and explains the risk. Do not run it. Close the chat when done.

Key takeaway

Remember it

CoCo is the library's robot assistant: it knows the map, wears your badge, and writes the request slips. Cortex AI reads the books. You read every slip before it is handed in.

Glossary

CoCo
Snowflake's AI coding agent, formerly called Cortex Code.
Agent
An AI that plans and carries out several steps toward a goal.
Prompt
The instruction you type to CoCo.
Metadata
Data about data: names of databases, tables and columns, and their types.
Role
Your ID badge in Snowflake. It decides what you, and CoCo, may see and do.
Token
A small piece of text an AI model reads or writes. CoCo is billed by tokens.
Cortex AI
Snowflake's AI features that work on data, such as AI_CLASSIFY and Cortex Analyst.
CoWork
The new name for Snowflake Intelligence: plain-English questions for business users.
