LLM basics: tokens, models, cost

Understand what a large language model is, how it counts text in tokens, how to pick a model and how Snowflake turns tokens into a bill you can predict and track.

Definition

Key definition

A large language model (LLM) is a computer program that has read a huge amount of text and learned to predict the next piece of text. Given your words, it keeps predicting the next piece until the answer is complete.

That simple trick, “guess the next piece, again and again”, is enough to summarise, translate, classify and answer questions. The model does not look facts up unless you give it the facts in the prompt (or connect it to search, as in Lesson 3.1). That is why it can sound sure and still be wrong.

  • Token: the piece of text a model reads and writes. Roughly four characters of English, so 100 words is about 130 tokens. The exact count varies by model.
  • Context window: the most tokens a model can hold at once, prompt plus answer.
  • Model: one trained LLM, such as llama3.1-8b or claude-sonnet-4-6. Bigger models reason better and cost more.

Think of it like a reader paid per word

Our smart-reader librarian is paid by the word: every word she reads from your request and every word she writes back. She works at a desk of fixed size. If you pile more pages on the desk than fit, she refuses the job. Some readers are juniors (fast, cheap, fine for sorting letters); some are seniors (slower, pricier, better at tricky reasoning). A small dial on her desk sets how creative she is allowed to be.

In the libraryWords she is paid forIn Snowflake: tokens, billed in AI Credits per million tokens
In the libraryThe size of her deskIn Snowflake: the context window, from 32,000 to 1,000,000 tokens by model
In the libraryJunior or senior readerIn Snowflake: small, medium or large models you pass to AI_COMPLETE

Count before you spend: AI_COUNT_TOKENS returns the input token count for a given function and model. It runs in every region and costs only warehouse time, no AI Credits.

SELECT AI_COUNT_TOKENS('ai_complete', 'llama3.1-8b',
       'My personal loan EMI was debited twice this month.') AS tokens;

SELECT AI_COUNT_TOKENS('ai_classify', 'I was charged an annual fee.',
       ['Fees and charges', 'Card issues', 'Fraud']) AS tokens;

How the bill works

Cortex AI Functions bill in AI Credits per million tokens, listed in the Snowflake Service Consumption Table. The query also needs a warehouse, which bills normal credits while it runs. Snowflake advises a warehouse no larger than MEDIUM: bigger ones do not make AI calls faster.

Function or modelInputOutputBilling note
AI_COMPLETE with mistral-7b0.090.12Input and output
AI_COMPLETE with llama3.1-8b0.1320.132Input and output
AI_COMPLETE with claude-haiku-4-50.603.00Input and output
AI_COMPLETE with claude-sonnet-4-61.809.00Input and output
AI_SENTIMENT1.60Snowflake-managed model
AI_CLASSIFY / AI_FILTER1.62Labels and descriptions count as input on every row

Read the numbers carefully

Figures are AI Credits per one million tokens from the Service Consumption Table effective 12 October 2026. On demand, one AI Credit lists at USD 2.00 (Global) or USD 2.20 (Regional). Prices change, so always check the current table before you quote a client.

Worked example. 40,000 complaints, each about 150 input tokens, and you ask AI_COMPLETE for a 50-token reply. That is 6 million input and 2 million output tokens. With llama3.1-8b: 6 × 0.132 + 2 × 0.132 ≈ 1.06 AI Credits. With claude-sonnet-4-6: 6 × 1.80 + 2 × 9.00 = 28.8 AI Credits, about 27 times more. Same job, very different bill: choosing the model is a cost decision.

Cross-region inference and tracking usage

Each model is hosted in certain regions. The account parameter CORTEX_ENABLED_CROSS_REGION decides whether a request may be processed elsewhere. Your data stays stored in your home region; only the prompt and answer travel for the duration of the call, and nothing is kept in the processing region. Credits are billed in your account’s region and there is no egress charge.

ValueMeaning
ANY_REGIONAny Snowflake region on any cloud. Widest model choice. Default for new organisations created after 9 March 2026.
AWS_GLOBAL, AZURE_GLOBAL, GCP_GLOBALStay within one cloud provider
AWS_US, AWS_EU, AWS_APJ, AZURE_EU …Stay within one cloud and one geography
DISABLEDHome region only. Strictest residency, fewest models.

Only ACCOUNTADMIN can change it. On a bank project this is a data-residency decision for the client, never yours.

-- Credits and tokens per function and model, last 30 days (view latency: up to 5 minutes)
SELECT DATE_TRUNC('day', start_time) AS usage_day, function_name, model_name,
       SUM(credits) AS credits
FROM SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.CORTEX_AI_FUNCTIONS_USAGE_HISTORY
WHERE start_time >= DATEADD('day', -30, CURRENT_TIMESTAMP())
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
ORDER BY usage_day DESC, credits DESC;

-- All AI services in one daily number
SELECT usage_date, credits_used
FROM SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.METERING_DAILY_HISTORY
WHERE service_type = 'AI_SERVICES';

In practice: a bank

A card team wants a short reply drafted for every complaint. The data scientist tests three models on 200 sample complaints, scoring each reply with a simple checklist (polite, correct amount, no promises). llama3.1-8b passes 88%, claude-haiku-4-5 95%, claude-sonnet-4-6 97%. Haiku costs a fraction of Sonnet for 2 points less, so the team picks Haiku, sets temperature to 0 and max_tokens to 120, and reviews the monthly bill in the usage view. That is the whole cost discipline in one paragraph.

Lab: tokens and models on your trial

Measure before you spend

About 15 minFree trial (Enterprise)

  1. Count tokens for one complaint with two different functions.

    SELECT AI_COUNT_TOKENS('ai_complete', 'llama3.1-8b', 'I paid my bill on the due date but was charged a late fee of Rs 1,200.') AS complete_tokens,
       AI_COUNT_TOKENS('ai_sentiment', 'I paid my bill on the due date but was charged a late fee of Rs 1,200.') AS sentiment_tokens;

    Expected: two small numbers. They can differ because each function counts its own way.

  2. Ask for the details of a call so you see the real token use.

    SELECT AI_COMPLETE(
  model => 'llama3.1-8b',
  prompt => 'In one sentence, what is a home loan EMI?',
  model_parameters => {'temperature': 0, 'max_tokens': 60},
  show_details => TRUE) AS details;

    Expected: JSON with choices, model and usage holding prompt_tokens, completion_tokens and total_tokens.

  3. See which models your account can call today.

    SHOW CORTEX BASE MODELS;

    Expected: a list of models with lifecycle details. If a model you need is missing, check the cross-region setting from Lesson 1.1.

  4. Wait about five minutes, then run the usage query from the section above. Find your AI_COMPLETE call and its credits.

    Expected: a row for AI_COMPLETE with model llama3.1-8b and a very small credit number.

Key takeaway

Remember

Everything in Cortex is measured in tokens. Count them first, choose the smallest model that passes your quality checks, keep temperature at 0 for factual work and track the real bill in the usage view.

Glossary

Token
A chunk of text the model reads or writes; about four English characters.
Context window
The maximum tokens one call can hold, input plus output.
Temperature
Randomness dial from 0 to 1. Default 0 in AI_COMPLETE.
max_tokens
The cap on output length for one AI_COMPLETE call. Default 4096.
AI Credit
The billing unit for AI features, priced per million tokens or per page.
Cross-region inference
Letting Cortex process a request in another region while data stays stored at home.
Legacy model
A retiring model that only accounts which already used it may keep calling until end-of-life.
AI_COUNT_TOKENS
Function that counts input tokens for a function and model without AI cost.
