Classify, filter and sentiment

Three task functions that turn free text into columns you can count: AI_CLASSIFY sorts, AI_FILTER answers yes or no, AI_SENTIMENT reads the mood, overall and per topic.

30 min Intermediate Practice worksheet + 3 challenges 2 simulators + lab + project step P2

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Classify text with AI_CLASSIFY using labels, descriptions, a task description and multi-label mode
  • Use AI_FILTER with PROMPT() in SELECT and WHERE
  • Read overall and aspect sentiment from AI_SENTIMENT
  • Build the COMPLAINTS.ENRICHED table with CATEGORY, SENTIMENT and IS_URGENT

Definition

Key definition

AI_CLASSIFY puts text into categories you name. AI_FILTER returns TRUE or FALSE for a yes/no question about text. AI_SENTIMENT returns the mood of text (positive, negative, neutral, mixed or unknown) overall and for up to ten aspects you list.

All three use a Snowflake-managed model, so you do not pick one, and each already contains a tuned prompt. You give the labels, the question or the aspects. They are cheaper to get right than AI_COMPLETE and their output shape is fixed, which makes them ideal for nightly pipelines.

Think of it like a mail room

Picture the bank’s mail room. One clerk drops each letter into one of six labelled trays (classify). A second clerk stamps a red URGENT mark on letters that mention fraud or lost money (filter). A third reads each letter and puts a smiley, a frown or a straight face sticker on it, and separate stickers for “fees”, “staff” and “app” if those are mentioned (sentiment with aspects).

In the libraryLabelled traysIn Snowflake: AI_CLASSIFY with your list of categories
In the libraryRed URGENT stampIn Snowflake: AI_FILTER returning TRUE or FALSE
In the libraryMood stickersIn Snowflake: AI_SENTIMENT, overall plus up to ten aspects

AI_CLASSIFY in depth

AI_CLASSIFY( <input>, <list_of_categories> [, <config_object>] [, <return_error_details>] )
-- returns {"labels": ["..."]}
PartRules from the docs
CategoriesAt least two unique values. Plain strings, or objects with label and an optional description of up to 25 words. More than about 20 categories can reduce accuracy. Labels are case sensitive.
task_descriptionUp to 50 words explaining the job. Helps when the link between text and labels is not obvious.
output_mode'single' (default, exactly one label) or 'multi' (one or more labels).
examplesFew-shot list: each has input, labels and explanation.
CostLabels, descriptions and examples are input tokens on every row, not once per query.
Best resultsEnglish text and labels, descriptive labels (not “cat1”), mutually exclusive categories.
 
-- Multi-label: a late fee on a card can be both topics
SELECT AI_CLASSIFY(
  'I paid my credit card bill on time but you charged a late payment fee.',
  ['Fees and charges', 'Card issues', 'Fraud'],
  {'output_mode': 'multi'}):labels AS LABELS;
-- ['Fees and charges', 'Card issues']

AI_FILTER in depth

AI_FILTER returns a BOOLEAN, so it works anywhere SQL expects TRUE or FALSE: in WHERE, in CASE, or as a column. Use PROMPT() to place the row text inside a clear question.

-- As a WHERE clause: keep only refund requests
SELECT COMPLAINT_ID, COMPLAINT_TEXT
FROM BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.RAW_COMPLAINTS
WHERE AI_FILTER(PROMPT('Is the customer asking for money back in this complaint? {0}', COMPLAINT_TEXT));

-- As a column you can count
SELECT PRODUCT,
       COUNT_IF(AI_FILTER(PROMPT('Does this complaint mention a fee or charge? {0}', COMPLAINT_TEXT))) AS FEE_MENTIONS
FROM BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.RAW_COMPLAINTS
GROUP BY PRODUCT;
Write the question well

The docs advise plain English, a question form and details: not “sounds angry” but “In the following bank complaint, does the customer threaten to close the account?”. Also keep NULLs out of the column you pass in.

AI_SENTIMENT in depth

FeatureDetail
OutputObject with a categories array. Each item has name and sentiment. The overall item is always included and comes first.
Labelspositive, negative, neutral, mixed and, for aspects not mentioned, unknown.
AspectsUp to 10 names, each up to 30 characters, for example ['fees', 'staff', 'app'].
LanguagesEnglish, French, German, Hindi, Italian, Spanish and Portuguese.
SizeOptimised for inputs up to 2,048 tokens (about 1,600 words).
 
SELECT AI_SENTIMENT(
  'The branch staff were very kind but the minimum balance charge of Rs 590 is unfair.',
  ['staff', 'fees', 'app']) AS S;
-- {"categories":[{"name":"overall","sentiment":"mixed"},
--   {"name":"staff","sentiment":"positive"},{"name":"fees","sentiment":"negative"},
--   {"name":"app","sentiment":"unknown"}]}
Old function, different answer

SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX.SENTIMENT returns a number from -1 to 1. AI_SENTIMENT returns labels. Do not mix them in one report.

In practice: a bank

A bank’s complaints head wants three things on Monday morning: how many complaints per topic, how angry customers are per product, and a list of urgent cases that must be called back today. That is exactly one AI_CLASSIFY, one AI_SENTIMENT and one AI_FILTER per row. The urgent list is not left to the model alone: the team also checks every TRUE row by hand for the first month and tracks how many were really urgent (precision) and how many urgent cases the filter missed (recall).

Project step P2: Classify and score sentiment

Complaint Intelligence · P2

We create BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.ENRICHED: every column of RAW_COMPLAINTS plus CATEGORY (VARCHAR, one of six labels), SENTIMENT (VARCHAR, overall label) and IS_URGENT (BOOLEAN). P3 adds more columns to this same table.

1. Build the table. One statement, three AI calls per row, 60 rows: a few thousand tokens in total.

USE WAREHOUSE LEARN_WH;
USE SCHEMA BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS;

CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.ENRICHED AS
SELECT r.COMPLAINT_ID, r.CUSTOMER_ID, r.PRODUCT, r.CHANNEL, r.SUBMITTED_AT, r.COMPLAINT_TEXT,
       -- 1. Topic: one label from six, with short descriptions to remove doubt
       AI_CLASSIFY(r.COMPLAINT_TEXT, [
         {'label': 'Fees and charges', 'description': 'annual fees, penalties, GST, processing, foreclosure or minimum balance charges'},
         {'label': 'Card issues',      'description': 'card limits, PIN, delivery, blocking by the bank, rewards, contactless or EMI conversion'},
         {'label': 'Loan servicing',   'description': 'EMIs, interest rates, statements, certificates, closure documents or recovery calls'},
         {'label': 'App and UPI',      'description': 'mobile app, login, UPI payments, transfers and recharges'},
         {'label': 'Customer service', 'description': 'staff behaviour, waiting time, branch or call centre requests, account updates'},
         {'label': 'Fraud',            'description': 'transactions or loans the customer did not make, scams, stolen money'}
       ], {'task_description': 'Classify a retail bank customer complaint by its main topic'}
       ):labels[0]::STRING AS CATEGORY,
       -- 2. Overall mood: positive, negative, neutral or mixed
       AI_SENTIMENT(r.COMPLAINT_TEXT):categories[0]:sentiment::STRING AS SENTIMENT,
       -- 3. Urgent: fraud, money lost or blocked access
       AI_FILTER(PROMPT('In this bank complaint, does the customer report fraud or a scam, money lost or debited without the service, or being blocked from their account, card or app? Complaint: {0}', r.COMPLAINT_TEXT)) AS IS_URGENT
FROM BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.RAW_COMPLAINTS r;

2. Check it.

SELECT CATEGORY, COUNT(*) AS COMPLAINTS,
       COUNT_IF(IS_URGENT) AS URGENT,
       COUNT_IF(SENTIMENT = 'negative') AS NEGATIVE
FROM BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.ENRICHED
GROUP BY CATEGORY
ORDER BY COMPLAINTS DESC;

Expected: six categories adding to 60, with App and UPI and Loan servicing the largest, and most Fraud rows urgent. Your exact counts may differ by one or two from the worksheet: that is normal for AI labels. Read every row where CATEGORY looks wrong.

3. Note for P3. AI_CLASSIFY works best on English. Rows 20, 32, 51 and 59 are Hindi or Hinglish; after P3 adds TEXT_EN you can re-run the classification on the English text for those rows.

Key takeaway

Remember

Use the task functions before AI_COMPLETE: AI_CLASSIFY for topics, AI_FILTER for yes/no questions, AI_SENTIMENT for mood. Clear labels, descriptions and questions matter more than anything else, and every label is billed on every row.

Glossary

AI_CLASSIFY
Sorts text, images or documents into the categories you list. Returns {"labels": [...]}.
Multi-label
output_mode multi: a row may get more than one label.
AI_FILTER
Returns TRUE or FALSE for a natural-language condition. Usable in WHERE.
AI_SENTIMENT
Returns mood labels overall and for up to ten aspects.
Aspect
A topic inside the text you want a separate mood for, such as fees or staff.
COUNT_IF
Snowflake function that counts rows where a condition is TRUE.
Precision / recall
Share of flagged rows that are really urgent / share of urgent rows that were flagged.
ENRICHED
Project table: raw complaints plus AI columns.
