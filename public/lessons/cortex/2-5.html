Embeddings and similarity

Turn every complaint into a list of numbers that captures its meaning, store it in a VECTOR column, and find complaints that mean the same thing even when they use different words.

Definition

Key definition

An embedding is a list of numbers (a vector) that an AI model produces to represent the meaning of a piece of text or an image. Texts with similar meaning get vectors that point in similar directions, so you can measure how alike two texts are with simple maths.

Keyword search fails when customers use different words: “someone took money with my OTP” and “fraudster drained my account” share almost no words but mean nearly the same. Embeddings compare meaning, not spelling. They are the engine under Cortex Search (Lesson 3.1), duplicate detection, clustering and “customers like this one” features.

Think of it like pins on a map of meaning

Imagine a giant map where every complaint gets a pin. Complaints about scams land in one neighbourhood, fee complaints in another, app problems in a third. The pin’s exact location is its coordinates; for a real model there are not 2 coordinates but 1,024. To find complaints like this one, you look for the nearest pins. Cosine similarity measures whether two arrows from the centre of the map point the same way: 1 means the same direction, 0 means unrelated.

Other distance functions

VECTOR_INNER_PRODUCT, VECTOR_L1_DISTANCE and VECTOR_L2_DISTANCE also exist. For text embeddings, cosine similarity is the usual choice. Vector functions can lose a little precision, up to about 0.0001.

In practice: a bank

A fraud operations team notices a new scam: callers asking customers to “confirm” a refund through a UPI collect request. Keyword rules catch only complaints containing the word “collect”. The team embeds every complaint each night. When an analyst marks one complaint as the new scam, a query lists the 50 most similar complaints from the last week, whatever words customers used. Within a day the team has a full picture, warns customers and blocks the merchant handles. The same table also flags near-duplicate complaints from one customer sent through two channels.

Lab: meaning beats keywords

Three quick experiments

About 10 minUses BANK_AI from P1 to P3

  1. Look at an embedding. It is just numbers.

    SELECT AI_EMBED('snowflake-arctic-embed-l-v2.0', 'My card was blocked') AS V;

    Expected: a long list of 1,024 small decimal numbers.

  2. Compare pairs with AI_SIMILARITY: same meaning, different words; then different meaning.

    SELECT AI_SIMILARITY('A scammer took money using my OTP', 'Fraudster drained my savings account') AS SAME_IDEA,
       AI_SIMILARITY('A scammer took money using my OTP', 'Please add a dark mode to the app') AS DIFFERENT;

    Expected: SAME_IDEA clearly higher than DIFFERENT.

  3. Search by meaning with your own words (run after P4).

    WITH q AS (SELECT AI_EMBED('snowflake-arctic-embed-l-v2.0', 'money left my account but the payment never reached') AS V)
SELECT e.COMPLAINT_ID, ROUND(VECTOR_COSINE_SIMILARITY(c.EMBEDDING, q.V), 3) AS SIM, e.TEXT_EN
FROM BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.COMPLAINT_EMBEDDINGS c
JOIN BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.ENRICHED e ON e.COMPLAINT_ID = c.COMPLAINT_ID, q
ORDER BY SIM DESC
LIMIT 5;

    Expected: failed or stuck payments such as 2, 20, 32 and 43 near the top, even though none uses your exact words.

Project step P4: Find similar complaints

Complaint Intelligence · P4

We create BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.COMPLAINT_EMBEDDINGS(COMPLAINT_ID NUMBER, EMBEDDING VECTOR(FLOAT, 1024)) with the model snowflake-arctic-embed-l-v2.0 on TEXT_EN, and a query that returns the 5 most similar complaints to any complaint.

USE WAREHOUSE LEARN_WH;
USE SCHEMA BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS;

-- 1. One 1024-number vector per complaint, from the English text (P3)
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.COMPLAINT_EMBEDDINGS (
  COMPLAINT_ID NUMBER,
  EMBEDDING    VECTOR(FLOAT, 1024)
);

INSERT INTO BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.COMPLAINT_EMBEDDINGS
SELECT COMPLAINT_ID,
       AI_EMBED('snowflake-arctic-embed-l-v2.0', TEXT_EN)
FROM BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.ENRICHED;

-- 2. The 5 complaints most similar to complaint 13 (OTP scam)
SELECT b.COMPLAINT_ID,
       ROUND(VECTOR_COSINE_SIMILARITY(a.EMBEDDING, b.EMBEDDING), 3) AS SIMILARITY,
       e.CATEGORY,
       e.TEXT_EN
FROM BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.COMPLAINT_EMBEDDINGS a
JOIN BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.COMPLAINT_EMBEDDINGS b ON b.COMPLAINT_ID <> a.COMPLAINT_ID
JOIN BANK_AI.COMPLAINTS.ENRICHED e            ON e.COMPLAINT_ID = b.COMPLAINT_ID
WHERE a.COMPLAINT_ID = 13
ORDER BY SIMILARITY DESC
LIMIT 5;

Expected: 60 rows in COMPLAINT_EMBEDDINGS; the top 5 for complaint 13 are fraud complaints such as 5, 58, 25, 46 and 39 with similarity roughly between 0.6 and 0.75. Change 13 to 2 to see failed-payment neighbours.

Key takeaway

Remember

An embedding turns meaning into numbers. Embed each text once with AI_EMBED, store it in a VECTOR column, and compare with VECTOR_COSINE_SIMILARITY to find complaints that mean the same thing, whatever words customers used.

Glossary

Embedding
A vector of numbers that represents the meaning of text or an image.
Vector
An ordered list of numbers; in Snowflake the VECTOR data type, up to 4,096 elements.
Dimension
How many numbers a vector holds, such as 768 or 1024.
AI_EMBED
Creates an embedding with the model you name.
Cosine similarity
How closely two vectors point the same way: 1 same, 0 unrelated, -1 opposite.
AI_SIMILARITY
Embeds two inputs and returns their cosine similarity in one call.
Semantic search
Finding text by meaning rather than exact words.
COMPLAINT_EMBEDDINGS
Project table with one 1024-number vector per complaint.
