Dates in depth

Month-end reports, account age, loans older than five years, days between two events. Almost every banking question has a date in it. This lesson makes calendar maths in SQL feel like counting on a calendar.

30 min Intermediate Practice: 6 questions

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Filter a date range the safe way, with >= and <
  • Pull out year and month, and bucket rows by month
  • Add days or years to a date, and count days between two dates
  • Translate between portable SQL and Snowflake’s DATEADD, DATEDIFF, LAST_DAY

Definition

Key definition

A DATE is a calendar day with no time of day. SQL can compare dates, extract parts of them, move them forward or back, and measure the gap between two of them.

Write a date literal as DATE '2026-09-30', year-month-day. This course uses a fixed “today”, DATE '2026-10-01', so every answer stays the same no matter when you run it. On a real project you would use CURRENT_DATE.

Think of it like counting on a wall calendar

Put your finger on 10 September. “Which month?” is reading the big word at the top: that is EXTRACT. “Flip to the first square of this page” is DATE_TRUNC to month. “Count 30 squares forward” is adding an interval. “How many squares between this and that?” is subtracting two dates. Nothing here is harder than a wall calendar; SQL just does the counting for you.

Syntax step by step

Step 1: a date range. Use >= for the start and < for the day after the end. This never misses the last day and works even when the column later becomes a timestamp.

SELECT txn_id, txn_date, amount
FROM transactions
WHERE txn_date >= DATE '2026-09-01'
  AND txn_date <  DATE '2026-10-01';

Step 2: parts and buckets.

SELECT cust_name,
       EXTRACT(YEAR FROM joined_on)  AS join_year,
       EXTRACT(MONTH FROM joined_on) AS join_month,
       CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', joined_on) AS DATE) AS join_month_start
FROM customers;

Step 3: moving and measuring.

SELECT acct_id, opened_on,
       opened_on + INTERVAL 30 DAY        AS review_due,
       LAST_DAY(opened_on)                AS first_statement,
       DATE '2026-10-01' - opened_on      AS days_open
FROM accounts;

Worked examples

Example 1: deposits per month. Bucket, then total. Cast the bucket to DATE so it prints as a date everywhere.

SELECT CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', txn_date) AS DATE) AS month_start,
       SUM(amount) AS total_deposits
FROM transactions
WHERE txn_type = 'DEPOSIT'
GROUP BY CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', txn_date) AS DATE)
ORDER BY month_start;

Result: July 1,45,000; August 6,42,000; September 3,47,000.

Example 2: loans older than five years on our fixed today. Add five years to the start and compare.

SELECT loan_id, start_date
FROM loans
WHERE start_date + INTERVAL 5 YEAR < DATE '2026-10-01';

Result: 7001 (2021-04-01) and 7003 (2019-02-01). On Snowflake: DATEADD(year, 5, start_date) < CURRENT_DATE.

Example 3: days between first and last transaction per customer. Aggregate the dates, then subtract.

SELECT c.cust_name, MIN(t.txn_date) AS first_txn, MAX(t.txn_date) AS last_txn,
       MAX(t.txn_date) - MIN(t.txn_date) AS days_span
FROM customers c
JOIN accounts a ON a.cust_id = c.cust_id
JOIN transactions t ON t.acct_id = a.acct_id
GROUP BY c.cust_id, c.cust_name;

Result: Anil Kapoor (id 1) spans 90 days, from 2 July to 30 September. Anil Kapoor (id 11) has one transaction, so his span is 0.

Example 4: customers who joined in the last 2 years. Move today back, then compare.

SELECT cust_name, joined_on
FROM customers
WHERE joined_on >= DATE '2026-10-01' - INTERVAL 2 YEAR;

Result: 3 customers: Jay Mehta (2025-02-11), Anil Kapoor (2025-09-01) and Kavya Iyer (2026-03-03).

Common mistakes

BETWEEN and the last day

BETWEEN '2026-09-01' AND '2026-09-30' is fine for a DATE column. The day the column becomes a TIMESTAMP, 30 September 14:05 is after 30 September 00:00 and falls out. The >= start AND < next_day form is right in both cases, so make it a habit.

Functions on the column kill filters

WHERE EXTRACT(YEAR FROM txn_date) = 2026 must compute the year for every row before it can filter. On a billion-row Snowflake table that means scanning everything. Write the range instead: txn_date >= DATE '2026-01-01' AND txn_date < DATE '2027-01-01'. Lesson 3.7 explains why this matters for pruning.

Dates stored as text

If a column holds '15/03/2019' as VARCHAR, comparisons sort as text and '15/03/2019' comes after '01/12/2026'. Convert once: CAST(col AS DATE) for ISO text, or Snowflake’s TO_DATE(col, 'DD/MM/YYYY') for other layouts, ideally when the data is loaded.

On Snowflake

The names you will see in bank code

DATEADD(day, 30, d) adds; DATEDIFF(day, d1, d2) counts whole units from d1 to d2 (also month, year); DATE_TRUNC('month', d) and LAST_DAY(d) bucket; TO_DATE(text, 'DD/MM/YYYY') parses; TO_CHAR(d, 'YYYY-MM') formats; CURRENT_DATE is today. Careful with DATEDIFF(year, …): it counts year boundaries crossed, so 31 Dec to 1 Jan is 1 year. Use day counts when precision matters. Snowflake also has DAYOFWEEK and WEEK, but the start of the week depends on an account setting, WEEK_START; check it before trusting weekly reports. Date subtraction d2 - d1 returns days on both engines.

Practice

Tables: customers, accounts, transactions and loans. Treat DATE '2026-10-01' as today. Write date literals as DATE 'YYYY-MM-DD'. Cast DATE_TRUNC results to DATE.

Key takeaway

Remember

Ranges use >= start and < next day. EXTRACT reads a part, DATE_TRUNC jumps to the first of the month, INTERVAL moves a date, and subtracting two dates gives days. On Snowflake the same jobs are DATEADD, DATEDIFF, DATE_TRUNC and LAST_DAY.

Glossary

DATE
A calendar day with no time part, written 'YYYY-MM-DD'.
TIMESTAMP
A date plus a time of day.
EXTRACT
Reads one part of a date, such as the year or month.
DATE_TRUNC
Rounds a date down to the start of a unit, such as the first of the month.
INTERVAL
A length of time you can add to or subtract from a date, such as INTERVAL 30 DAY.
LAST_DAY
The last calendar day of the month a date falls in.
DATEADD / DATEDIFF
Snowflake’s functions for moving a date and for counting units between two dates.
Half-open range
A filter of the form >= start AND < end, where end is excluded.
